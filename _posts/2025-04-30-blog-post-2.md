## Rigor Mortis
During my time at KULeuven, we had a course in Linear Models. The course was taught by two great professors, and, perhaps unsurprisingly, one of them was both a DJ, and the friendliest human being you could have as a teacher in Statistics[^1]. 
While being a mandatory course with a reputation of being simple but not simplistic, explaining orally your thought process for something that'd normally require a bunch of equations and R output, is a skill in itself that you better not underestimate. Look too hesitant? You fail, so keep your onomatopoeia for your inner self.

In retrospect, this course had the most impact on my early career, since I completely changed my approach to study and apply statistics.
I'm not just talking about passing exam here, but my own research career, since the rigor I learned throughout my cursus made me realize I had not other choice but to stop my research project of the time and start everything over, regardless of the consequences on my reputation.
> _"Your reputation is harmed the most by what you say to defend it."_ — N. N. Taleb
