No, this blog won't be generated in any shape or form by a Large Language Model <small>(hum.. it's not quite the whole truth. I will let a "LLM" check my grammar, because posterity)</small>. There's a place for that, and it's called Linkedin.

## "Always take the long path, avoid shortcuts"

The main reason I prohibit myself from using it in this case (though I’ve found some uses elsewhere, which I’ll share later) is that we substitute our thinking with a model developed by people who didn’t use any. Over time, it will have the same effect as automatic correction on our phones — you’ll struggle to write by yourself, double-thinking the grammar of every word.

Another reason is that I’m more interested in sharing insights earned through experience. While _“AI” agents_ are now able to display the steps taken to a solution, they still lack the tinkering aspect that we can only encounter while doing — the aspect that gives depth to a solution rather than just a superficial-yet-correct answer.

And last but not least, I enjoy sharing a part of my personality in the way I frame my thoughts — not just through the content, but the form. In the end, hasn’t it been known for centuries that a great writer will transcend a persona, whether their own or an invented one, through semantics alone?

> “To play a wrong note is insignificant; to play without passion is inexcusable.”  
> _— attributed to Beethoven_

I'm still learning — do not expect me to avoid mistakes at the wrong time, or better, the right time. Don’t expect me not to rediscover the wheel either. Just don’t kill the fun — it's my driver toward excellence.
The information encoded in failure is generally much more salient than that in success, meaning you can't truly compete without failing a few times.  
In other words, I'm happy to fail, as long as I don't lose.

## What can you expect from me?

I don’t make empty promises — you already hear enough of those elsewhere. But I *will* stick to posting once every two weeks. That means every post you read will have taken about one week to think through and test, and another to write.

If I were jobless and without a social life, I could reduce that to two days — but surprise, surprise, I’m not.

I suggest you start your own blog if you haven’t already. In the end, anything carefully written and coming from you will be engraved in your memory forever.  
If you do, and you’d like to share it, feel free to drop the link in the comments below. I’ll gladly read whatever drives your passion — serendipity is always somewhere unexpected, by definition.

> _"If you are what you eat, then I only want to eat the good stuff."_  
> — attributed to Remy, in *Ratatouille*
