This paper, that was part of my master thesis, aims to show the impact of the thematic content of ESG reports on information asymmetry between managers and investors. Using a sentence-based topic modeling algorithm, the authors determine and empirically quantify 30 topics in a large collection of 1,667 ESG reports between 2007 and 2020.

The analysis shows that:
- The algorithm produces a semantically meaningful set of topics.
- There are clear patterns and temporal shifts in the focus on environmental, social, or governance issues.
- ESG report content reduces information asymmetry around and after the release date.
- Investors react more to environmental topics, while social and governance topics are perceived as less informative.
- There is a concave relationship between the number of topics in ESG reports and their informativeness.

The paper contributes theoretically by demonstrating that investor reactions are sensitive not only to the presence of ESG disclosures but also to their thematic content.
